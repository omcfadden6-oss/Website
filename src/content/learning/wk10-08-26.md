### More on the Delta Graph
After some research, I discovered my approach wasn’t a very clean approach. Because F1 cars send pings at slightly different co-ordinates, doing comparisons are a bit more tricky - subtracting their times is not accurate because they are not at the same point on the track. 

Instead, I learned a cleaner approach;
- Use a linspace to create a uniform distance grid for accurate comparison (using the maximum distance of the shortest lap - use the shortest to avoid interpolation errors)
- Convert the time data to total seconds
- Interpolate each lap time against the uniform distance grid - ie use the existing lap time array to calculate what the time would be at the specified distance points in the uniform distance grid array
- Calculate the delta by subtracting Kimi’s time from Max’s (graph in the negatives means that Max is down to Kimi)

I encountered a problem - everything ran smoothly, but the numbers were off. According to my code, Max was 0.255 seconds ahead of Kimi - I knew that was not accurate. I checked the timing data of both drivers to make sure that they were at 0 at the start of the lap (they were), but it wasn’t until I checked the distance data that I found the problem - Kimi’s telemetry stopped earlier than Max’s, meaning the delta stopped being calculated before the lap was over for Max. The difference in lap distance for the 2 drivers was actually around 22 metres. This meant the delta was skewed, because the drivers were being compared at mismatched positions around the physical circuit.

This difference between the data sets can arise because FastF1 reconstructs the distance from the telemetry samples, and the sampling/position data differ between laps. 

Because the distance values for each driver do not match up, I created a normalised distance co-ordinate so that the delta is calculated in terms of the drivers progress on the lap rather than the mismatched data.

## My main takeaway from the week
Plotting the delta graph taught me a lot about how the data is obtained by FastF1 - knowing that the telemetry for each driver does not have identical sampling rates has definitely stayed in the back of my mind, and made me more careful when thinking about how best to compare the drivers in certain plots. For example, I did a quick plot of both racing lines through turns 5 and 6, but knowing that the sampling rates are different (and sometimes lower), I felt it was best to use a scatter plot, rather than a line plot, so that there are no inaccuracies created by Python trying to fill in the gaps between the data.
